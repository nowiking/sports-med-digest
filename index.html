J ISAKOS · 2026-11-03 · 随机对照试验 · PMID 42976660

一句话结论：在50例拟行关节镜下肩袖修复的患者中，4-6周肩胛肌预康复虽安全可行，但术后6个月VAS、ASES、SPADI、Constant等评分及喙突压痛与对照组无显著差异。

要点：两组术后各时间点患者报告结局均较术前显著改善；预康复组在肩胛肌力量对称性恢复上未显示优势；喙突压痛发生率组间无差异；未报告预康复相关不良事件。

对临床的启示：该阴性结果提示，对所有肩袖撕裂患者常规开展术前肩胛肌预康复可能并非必要，资源应优先投向肩胛运动障碍显著或延迟手术的亚组。需注意样本量仅50例、随访6个月，可能不足以检出小效应；预康复依从性与剂量未标准化亦限制推广。临床决策应个体化，而非一刀切。

Conclusion: A 4- to 6-week preoperative scapular-focused prehabilitation program did not significantly improve 6-month functional outcomes or reduce coracoid pain after arthroscopic rotator cuff repair compared with usual care.

Key points: Both groups improved significantly across all patient-reported outcomes; no between-group differences were observed in VAS, ASES, SPADI, Constant, EQ-5D-5L, or SICK scores; coracoid tenderness did not differ; no prehabilitation-related adverse events occurred.

Clinical implications: Routine preoperative scapular muscle training for all rotator cuff repair patients may not be warranted; resources should target subgroups with marked scapular dyskinesis or delayed surgery. We retrospectively reviewed patients who underwent OWHTO for varus medial compartment knee osteoarthritis between 2018 and 2020 and had preoperative MRI and ≥2-year follow-up. Carbohydrate is the principal limiting fuel for prolonged, high-intensity endurance exercise, and consensus guidelines have long capped recommended intake during exercise at approximately 90 g/h. Two developments have challenged and extended contemporary fueling practice: a shift toward carbohydrate intakes of 100 to 150 g/h in elite endurance sport, and widespread commercial availability of exogenous ketone supplements marketed as ergogenic aids. Both have outpaced the clinical evidence and have reached the competitive and recreational athletes who present to sports medicine clinicians. Narrative review of primary metabolic and performance studies, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and consensus statements published through mid-2026, identified through PubMed and reference tracing. Narrative review. Level 5. Multiple-transportable-carbohydrate formulations (glucose plus fructose) raise exogenous carbohydrate oxidation above the roughly 1 g/minute ceiling imposed by intestinal glucose transport, providing the mechanistic basis for fueling rates of ≥90 g/h. Ingesting 120 g/h increases exogenous carbohydrate oxidation, but the best-controlled studies have not demonstrated additional endogenous (liver and muscle) glycogen sparing or a consistent performance advantage, and gastrointestinal tolerability requires deliberate gut training. For exogenous ketones, an influential 2016 study reported altered substrate use and improved time-trial performance. Subsequent trials and multiple systematic reviews and meta-analyses have not confirmed an acute ergogenic effect, and several have documented gastrointestinal distress or impaired performance. The more promising and reproducible signal for ketones lies in postexercise recovery and attenuation of overreaching. Finally, athletes who restrict in-competition and training carbohydrate to pursue leanness risk low energy and carbohydrate availability, the substrate of Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs). Carbohydrate intake of ≤90 g/h, built through progressive gut training, is supported by consensus guidelines and mechanistic evidence for prolonged, high-intensity endurance events; progression toward 120 g/h is reasonable in selected, well-adapted athletes competing in the longest events, with the understanding that a performance benefit >90 g/h remains unproven. Routine exogenous ketone supplementation for acute performance is not supported by current evidence; a recovery-focused application is plausible and increasingly supported but requires further study.
