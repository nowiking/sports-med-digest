ACL 翻修加外侧增强：移植物生存率显著提高，残余旋转松弛更少

Lateral Extra-Articular Augmentation Is Associated With Improved Graft Survival After Revision Anterior Cruciate Ligament Reconstruction

J ISAKOS · 2026-09-26 · 三中心 300 例 · PMID 42800674

一句话结论：364 例首次单期翻修 ACLR 筛查，最终 300 例入组（单纯翻修 100、翻修 + LET 100、翻修 + ALL 重建 100），用逆概率治疗加权（IPTW）校正选择偏倚。外侧关节外增强（LET 或 ALL）与单纯翻修相比，移植物生存率显著更高、残余旋转松弛更少；LET 与 ALL 重建的直接比较为探索性分析。

要点：2015-01 至 2019-12 三个高流量膝关节中心连续病例，首次单期翻修 ACLR。以 IPTW 校正治疗分配偏倚，比较移植物生存、术后旋转松弛与患者报告结局，LET 与 ALL 重建的直接比较作为探索性终点。

对临床的启示：翻修 ACL 的失败率本就高于初次，而「翻修要不要加外侧增强」长期缺乏干净证据——因为做增强的患者往往本身风险更高。这篇用 IPTW 把选择偏倚压下去后，答案很清晰：外侧增强（无论 LET 还是 ALL）与更好的移植物生存、更少的旋转松弛相关。它和 017 期「后倾 ≥12° 加 LET 后不再是高危」、015 期 MacIntosh 髂胫束联合重建，串成同一条越来越完整的线索——前外侧结构，正在从「可选附加」变成「高危/翻修场景的常规配置」。局限是回顾性、观察性，且 LET vs ALL 谁更优仍无定论。

Conclusion: Of 364 screened first-time single-stage revision ACLRs, 300 were included (100 isolated, 100 +LET, 100 +ALL reconstruction), analyzed with inverse probability of treatment weighting. Lateral extra-articular augmentation (LET or ALL) was associated with significantly higher graft survival and less residual rotational laxity than isolated revision ACLR; the LET-versus-ALL comparison was exploratory.

Key points: Consecutive first-time single-stage revision ACLRs (2015–2019) at three high-volume centers; IPTW to adjust allocation bias; outcomes included graft survival, rotational laxity and PROMs, with LET-vs-ALL as an exploratory endpoint.

Clinical implications: Revision ACL already fails more than primary, and whether to add lateral augmentation has long lacked clean evidence—because augmented patients are typically higher-risk to begin with. After IPTW reduces that allocation bias, the answer is clear: lateral augmentation (LET or ALL) is associated with better graft survival and less rotational laxity. Together with Issue 017 (PTS ≥12° no longer high-risk with LET) and Issue 015 (MacIntosh ITB), a coherent thread emerges—the anterolateral structures are moving from 'optional add-on' to 'routine configuration' in high-risk/revision settings. Limitations: retrospective, observational, and LET-vs-ALL superiority remains unresolved.
